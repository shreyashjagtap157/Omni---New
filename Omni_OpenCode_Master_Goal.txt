Work in the local Omni-New repository as the primary OpenCode agent. Your mission is to research, design, implement, integrate, verify and qualify Omni as an idealized programming language and complete development platform. Treat the language, compiler, runtime, standard library, tools, installation experience, documentation and distribution as one product. “Idealized” means coherent, expressive, safe, predictable, efficient, usable and evolvable—not a language that accumulates features indiscriminately or copies other languages' limitations.

Follow a deliberately staged syntax strategy. FIRST complete Omni using the best practical, developer-familiar syntax for the current programming-language ecosystem. Do NOT make the initial language vibe-coded, conversational, sentence-like or arbitrary-natural-language-based. The first syntax must be modern, professional, concise, readable, consistent, easy to learn, easy to remember, pleasant to write and review, and familiar to developers moving from established languages. Research current developer adoption, admiration, desired-language data, established language conventions, empirical syntax-comprehension research, and feedback from credible engineering communities. Compare the strengths of widely used and well-regarded languages (including but not limited to Rust, TypeScript, Python, Go, Kotlin, C#, Swift and others relevant to each construct). Do not mistake language popularity or an “admired” survey score for a direct measurement of syntax preference. Use those as signals only; combine them with evidence about readability, familiarity, expressiveness, consistency, tooling, error prevention and learnability. Do not simply clone any language. Select and justify a coherent syntax for Omni from first principles and evidence, with examples of realistic programs and competing alternatives.

For this FIRST stage, favour established, recognizable programming constructs and conventions: clear declarations and types, functions, expressions, blocks, control flow, pattern matching, modules, generics, error handling, iteration, data modelling, concurrency and systems programming. Reduce unnecessary ceremony through good inference and concise APIs, but keep important operations, control flow, effects, ownership transfers, mutation, failures, resource lifetimes, unsafe behavior and nondeterminism visible. Define operator precedence and associativity, statement termination, comments, identifiers, literals, escaping, formatting and all ambiguity rules precisely. Optimize both writing and reading: code review, debugging, diagnostics, accessibility, editor support, diffs and long-term maintenance matter as much as typing fewer characters. Establish a canonical formatter and a stable grammar. Do not pursue novelty for its own sake.

ONLY AFTER the complete first-stage language and its agreed ecosystem have passed their qualification gates, begin the SECOND stage: research and design an optional, distinctly vibe-coded surface syntax that lets programmers express intent in highly natural, low-ceremony, potentially sentence-like forms. This is a later evolution, not part of the initial implementation target and not a reason to delay completion of the first stage. Do not implement or promote vibe-coded syntax prematurely. At the beginning, preserve a clean compiler architecture boundary so a later surface-syntax frontend can be added without destabilizing the completed language; do not build speculative infrastructure beyond what is justified. When the second stage is authorized by completion gates, design its own formal, deterministic grammar and map it into the same established semantic core. Preserve the first-stage syntax as a supported stable surface unless a separately approved compatibility and migration decision says otherwise. Vibe-coded must describe the programmer-facing syntax, never probabilistic compiler behavior: no arbitrary prose parsing, fuzzy NLP, LLM-dependent semantics, hidden conversational context or guesswork. Every accepted form must have deterministic parsing, binding, typing, diagnostics and desugaring; ambiguity must be rejected. Surface conveniences must never bypass types, ownership, lifetimes, effects, capabilities, safety or verification.

Do not treat an existing grammar or candidate as automatically optimal or authoritative for a new design. At the same time, preserve the repository's established specification authority, edition boundaries, manifest integrity, accepted ADRs and compatibility promises. Audit the actual status of Edition 1, Candidate 2, Candidate 3, parser profiles and all related documents. In particular, the repository has previously identified Candidate 2/Vibe proposals as unratified and ADR-0003 as keeping experimental syntax separate from the strict Edition 1 production profile; verify the current records and implementation. Never silently activate candidate syntax, change normative Edition 1 behavior, alter a manifest digest, or relabel a proposal as ratified. Develop improvements through the proper candidate/edition/ADR process. You are authorized to research and design the first-stage conventional syntax and later-stage vibe syntax within this roadmap, but changes that break established Edition 1 behavior, compatibility guarantees or fundamental safety commitments require the prescribed approval process and user intervention.
